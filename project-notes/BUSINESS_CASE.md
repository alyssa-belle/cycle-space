## 1. The idea

Cycle Space is a two sided market that connects property owners with establishments such as community groups, small businesses, trade schools, not for profit businesses, and more who need flexible spaces. Property owners could have too much space in their building, with a lot of square footage going unused, and other organizations could need space for weekly meetings, workshops, etc. without signing long term leases that are often not affordable. If someone wished to find and arrange spaces to rent or use, it would be a complicated process of finding the right person to get in touch with, worrying about if the space will be available for the next meeting, and trying to coordinate availability manually which leaves room for double booking mistakes. Cycle Space simplifies the process by facilitating the bookings for both property owners and potential renters in one platform. Property owners can earn additional income for unused space and access insights that help them understand what users want. Cycle Space generates its revenue from commissions on bookings, as well as optional analytical monthly subscriptions for owners who own multiple properties. 

## 2. Where IT creates value

IT creates value with Cycle Space through changing operational efficiency by bringing the rental process into one system and platform, instead of property owners and potential renters coordinating booking requests, insurance agreements, pricing, and if the spaces are available through multiple employees, emails, messages, and phone calls. All facilitation is done through one platform with a third party to deal with the meticulous details. Administrative work for both sides of the customer market is reduced, which saves them time to do other planning and more efficiently operate. This is especially beneficial for the non-profits, schools, and community organizations who need spaces, as it takes away the difficulty of outsourcing many different spaces for programming. Property owners can also manage bookings and generate revenue very easily through our platform. Cycle Space also uses technology to have a database of available commercial spaces which allows users to find properties based on their needs of locations, capacity, accessibility, and what a room includes (wifi, whiteboard, sink, etc.). The value comes from allowing properties to track booking history, revenue, and know how their space is being utilized. We also use technology to create a competitive advantage against other platforms, as we already have an established history with organizations. 

## 3. Network effects

Cycle Space operates as a two-sided market, connecting property owners with businesses and organizations looking for flexible spaces. The supply is property owners listing unused spaces, with the demand side being small businesses, trade schools, nonprofits, and other establishments looking to rent. 
The platform gains value as participation increases on each side. Having more property owners means a better variety of available spaces, giving renters more choices based on their wants. As well, more organizations searching for spaces will give property owners a greater pool of of renters and the opportunity to generate more revenue. It is a cross side network effect, when growth on one side increases value for the other side. Gaining data from bookings can also provide analytics on demand and how spaces are used. 
The cold start problem will be a challenge for Cycle Space. If we do not have initial listings, organizations have little reason to join, and property owners could be hesitant without confirmed renters. To address this challenge, Cycle Space would only start with one small geographic area instead of an entire province. Partnering with a couple of property owners to set up listings while also reaching out to local establishments, trade schools, and groups in the community to understand how much demand is there. After both supply and demand is understand for a certain area, Cycle Space could then expand into antoher location. 
On our homepage, there is a "List your space" option, and a "Explore available spaces" option. These options show both sides of the market and how both property owners and renters contribute to the business. 


## 4. Can it be copied? (the moat question)

Cycle Space as a startup/website could easily be copied if people use similar AI tools or know how to code. The website and business idea is not the strong part of this business, like most. Cycle Space needs to gain resources that become difficult for competitors to maintain. 
One advantage could be relationships with property owners. The relationships are valuable because owners will trust us, guves us acess to their spaces, and have booking be simplified. It could be rare depending on location if Cycle Space gains a reputation or connection with a town or city. But, people could copy the idea and decide to approach the same businesses for property or to get renters, so it's not impossible to imitate. Cycle Space will maintain effective relationships with its clients and be reliable for people to continue using it. 
A second advantage would be the booking history and data collected as the platform grows. We could know which spaces are being used for what programs, when demand is highest, and what spaces are most wanted. This is a valuable tool because it helps property owners make decisions on availability and pricing based on our specific data, which also means its rare and hard to imitate. People who copy the idea would need successful bookings and time to evaluate the data. We would need to have an organized enterprise system to collect and present the information. 
A final advantage could be could be developing specialized knowledge about commercial spaces and utilizing them in unique or unconventional ways. Such as a nonprofit using a commercial kitchen in a restaurant to make free meals for the community, or doing practical training for a trades program in a warehouse. This is valuable as it assists with identifying opportunities that property owners could have no thought of. Competitors could research similar opportunities, but Cycle Space could have an advantage of rarity as their data comes from real experience and already established processes for matching properties and establisments. 
If a competitor were to have a large budget they could easily copy the website and brand idea, advertise it more widely, and approach potential users with a good financial standing. Yet, developing relationships, gaining data from bookings, and getting experience with multiple property users would take time. Cycle Space proposed potential advantages would strengthen as time goes on and it grows, mostly through its expanding network and establishing booking history. The competitive advantage would not come from the website but from information, relationships and resources. 


## 5. Cloud and operations

To operate, the platform would need reliable cloud services to manage peoples accounts, property listings, requests for bookings, payment methods and information, and insurance related agreements. We could use a third party log-in and authentication system, whcih would be purchased as software as a service (SaaS), and it would allow for property owners and organizations to create accounts and manage information securely. We could also use Platform as a Service (PaaS) to run our website which could handle updates and infastructure without requiring Cycle Space to maintain its own servers. A cloud database could store property listings, availability, history, and customer information. Another potential option is having a payment system to handle transactions between establishments and property owners, reducing the need for us to manage to manage sensitive information. However, it would require working through the commissions first. Cycle Space would also require ongoing operations as people join the platform, like verifying listings, coordinating agreements, availability, and customer support if there's any issues. A risk for this would be relying too much on one provider. If a service we purchased experiences an outage or difficulties, customers could lose access to listings and renting. Also, if any of our providers increase their prices, it could effect how commissions are used. To ensure these risks do not become challenges we face, Cycle Space should keep up with regular data backups, and have alternate providers for servers available if needed. The platform should maintain reliable as it grows. 

## 6. Risks and ethics

One risk for Cycle Space is misusing or exposing information. If the platform collects names and information, organization details and locations, and potentially even payment information. Depending on what cloud and operations services we purhase, we could be handling this information. If this data were to be accessed or seen without permission, both sides of our market could be affected. Cycle Space would need to limit what information it collects and use secure providers, as well as generally restrict access to sensitive customer information. Another risk could be Cycle Space unintentionally creating unequal access if some establishments are priorized because they can afford higher rates. this could disadvantage smaller not for profits and community groups. If we use demand data to establish pricing and increase it during times where demand increases, this could lead to some organizations not being able to access it. Encouraging affordable listings and having a varied amount of pricings on different spaces would help to ensure that the platform also supports community-focused people who really benefit from the circular economy idea.
